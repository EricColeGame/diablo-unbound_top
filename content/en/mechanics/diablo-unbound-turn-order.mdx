## How Diablo Unbound Turn Order Structures a Round

Diablo Unbound is a cooperative tabletop experience set in the Diablo universe, and like most co-op dungeon crawlers it runs on a repeating loop: the table resolves upkeep, heroes take their activations, the opposition responds, and the round closes. Understanding that loop is the difference between a group that finishes a scenario in one sitting and one that spends the night flipping pages.

The key insight is that turn order is not just "who goes first." It is the scaffolding that tells you *when* an effect resolves, whether a reaction is legal, and which player makes the next decision. Once the sequence becomes second nature, players stop asking permission and start playing.

Most rounds follow this shape. Your printing's exact phase names may differ, so treat your copy of the rulebook as the final authority.

| Step | What happens | Who resolves it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round start | Timed effects tick down, tokens refresh, scenario triggers fire | Table, together | Skipping this silently breaks multi-round abilities |
| Hero activations | Each hero spends movement and actions in the agreed order | Individual players | This is where most tactical decisions live |
| Opposition phase | Monsters and hazards activate according to their instructions | One dedicated player or the table | Concentrating this keeps enemy behavior consistent |
| Round end | Cleanup, condition checks, objective scoring | Table, together | Prevents "I forgot to heal" disputes |

Two habits make this loop sing. First, announce each phase out loud — a simple "round start" and "opposition phase" callout keeps everyone synced. Second, resolve all effects of the same timing simultaneously rather than letting the loudest player jump the queue.

## Diablo Unbound Turn Order, Step by Step

If you are teaching new players, walk them through the sequence in the order below. It maps the round onto concrete decisions rather than abstract rules text.

1. **Call the round.** The table announces a new round and resolves any start-of-round effects in the order printed on the cards.
2. **Set the hero order.** Agree who activates first. Many groups rotate the lead each round so no single player always eats the first monster hit.
3. **Activate one hero at a time.** The active player declares movement, then actions, then any end-of-activation triggers.
4. **Resolve reactions immediately.** If a hero or monster has a trigger that fires during another activation, resolve it at the moment it qualifies — not at the end of the round.
5. **Run the opposition.** Move and attack with monsters in a consistent, declared order (left to right across the board works well).
6. **Close the round.** Tick down durations, discard spent tokens, check objective progress, then return to step 1.

| Decision point | Question to ask | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Hero order | Who benefits most from acting before the enemy? | Always letting the same player go first |
| Action split | Move then attack, or attack then move? | Declaring actions after seeing dice results |
| Reaction timing | Does this trigger right now, or later? | Banking reactions "for later" — they expire |
| Opposition order | Which monster activates next? | Choosing order reactively to punish one player |

The single most useful discipline in Diablo Unbound turn order is declaring intent before rolling. Say "I move here and attack that monster," then roll. It removes the temptation to reverse decisions after a bad result and keeps the sequence honest.

## Timing Windows: Interrupts, Reactions, and Monster Activations

Timing is where turn order gets interesting. Most disagreements at the table are not about *who* acts, but about *when* something happens relative to something else. Think of the round as a series of windows rather than a solid block.

| Timing window | Opens when | Closes when | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before an activation | The active player is chosen | They declare their first action | Pre-emptive buffs, repositioning |
| During an activation | An action is declared | The action fully resolves | Reactions, defensive triggers |
| After an activation | The active player ends their turn | The next player is chosen | Cleanup effects, "end of turn" abilities |
| Between rounds | Round end resolves | The next round is called | Persistent effects, scenario triggers |

A practical rule of thumb: if an effect says it happens "when" something occurs, it resolves inside that window; if it says "after," it waits until the window closes. Groups that internalize this distinction rarely need to pause play.

Player experience suggests the messiest moments come from defensive abilities that trigger on someone else's turn. Handle these by having the defending player keep their reaction card face-up and visible, so the active player can see the trigger coming and sequence their actions accordingly.

## Diablo Unbound Turn Order Tips for Co-op Tables

Cooperative games live or die on coordination, and turn order is the coordination tool. Assigning light table roles prevents the classic co-op failure mode where four players all try to run the same activation.

| Table role | Responsibility | Keeps the round moving by |
|---|---|---|
| Sequence caller | Announces each phase and names the next player | Eliminating "whose turn is it?" pauses |
| Rules keeper | Holds the rulebook, checks timing questions | Stopping mid-round searches |
| Monster handler | Runs opposition activations in a fixed order | Making enemy behavior predictable |
| Tracker | Manages tokens, conditions, and round counters | Preventing forgotten upkeep effects |

- **Rotate the lead.** Let a different player take the first hero activation each round; it spreads risk and keeps everyone engaged.
- **Batch your questions.** Collect rulebook questions and resolve them at round end rather than interrupting every activation.
- **Pre-plan as a team.** Spend thirty seconds at round start agreeing on priorities, then let players act independently.
- **Narrate the opposition.** A quick "the horde advances" while moving monsters keeps the table oriented and makes the turn order feel like a story beat.
- **Watch the action economy.** If one hero consistently has nothing to do by the time their activation arrives, the order needs adjusting.

Groups that treat Diablo Unbound turn order as a shared responsibility rather than a referee's job consistently report faster, smoother sessions — and fewer moments where someone realizes, too late, that they forgot to move.

## Common Turn Order Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

Even experienced tables slip. Here are the recurring problems and the fastest corrections.

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping round start | Buffs expire a round late | Read the round-start checklist aloud every time |
| Free-form hero order | Arguments over who declared first | Fix an order at the start of each round |
| Delayed reactions | "I meant to use that earlier" | Reactions resolve when triggered or not at all |
| Monster order chosen reactively | Enemies feel unfairly punishing | Declare monster order before rolling any dice |
| Ignoring end-of-round cleanup | Conditions pile up unnoticed | Assign one player as tracker |
| House rules nobody wrote down | Two versions of the same rule | Keep a one-page house rules sheet at the table |

If your group keeps hitting the same snag, it is usually a sequencing problem rather than a rules problem. Slow the round down for two sessions, call every phase, and the rhythm returns.

## FAQ

**Does Diablo Unbound turn order change with player count?**
The structure of the round stays the same, but hero activation order and the number of activations per round scale with the group. Confirm the player-count rules in your rulebook before your first session, since scenario setup often adjusts opposition behavior too.

**Who should run monster activations?**
One dedicated player is usually fastest, with the role rotating between sessions. What matters is that monster activation order is declared openly and applied consistently — that keeps Diablo Unbound turn order fair even when the opposition is being run by a single person.

**Can we house-rule Diablo Unbound turn order?**
Yes, and many groups do. Rotating initiative, simultaneous hero phases, or a fixed monster order are all common tweaks. Write down whatever you change so the whole table plays the same game, and revert the change if it makes scenarios trivially easy.

**Where do I look when a timing question comes up mid-game?**
Go to the rulebook's timing or sequence section first, then check the specific card text. If the answer is still unclear, make a temporary ruling, note it on your house rules sheet, and revisit it after the session so play never stalls.

**What is the fastest way to teach Diablo Unbound turn order to new players?**
Run one demo round with no monsters, calling each phase out loud. New players learn the sequence in about five minutes when they can focus on the loop instead of on survival.
