### Cinematic vs. Gameplay: How to Tell Them Apart

Blizzard-style cinematics are gorgeous and deliberately ambiguous. Use these tells to work out what you're actually looking at:

- **Camera freedom.** A camera that swoops through a battlefield without clipping is cinematic. A camera locked behind a character's shoulder is gameplay.
- **UI presence.** Health globes, action bars, and cooldown icons mean real footage. Their absence means nothing either way.
- **Hit reactions.** Enemies that stagger, ragdoll, and get knocked into geometry suggest simulation. Enemies that dissolve into scripted particles suggest a cutscene.
- **Crowd behavior.** Dozens of monsters pathing independently is expensive; a staged wave is cheap.
- **Labeling.** "In-engine" means the renderer is real, but lighting and post-effects are usually dialed up beyond what ships.

A useful rule: the closer the camera sits to a character's spine, the more likely you're seeing the actual game.

## Classes, Combat, and Systems to Expect

Class teases are the most over-read part of any reveal. A single armored shoulder can spawn a thousand forum threads. Watch for silhouettes rather than specifics.

| Archetype | What to look for in footage | Question worth asking |
|---|---|---|
| Melee bruiser | Heavy footfalls, shield or two-hander, ground slam | Is the combat weighty or floaty? |
| Ranged caster | Staff, orb, projectile trails, standing casts | Can they move while casting? |
| Agile rogue | Twin blades, dashes, afterimages | Is there a dodge with i-frames? |
| Summoner | Pets, totems, corpses rising | Do minions persist or expire? |
| Support or hybrid | Buff auras, chants, group positioning | Is solo play still viable? |
| Unidentified new class | Weapon type with no established moveset | Is this a genuine addition or a reskin? |

Beyond classes, pay attention to pacing. Fast cuts hide slow combat, so look for uninterrupted sequences of three seconds or more. Those longer shots reveal the resource system, the dodge cooldown, and whether encounters are built around packs or single elites. If the **Diablo Unbound trailer** shows a boss with telegraphed phases, that's a much stronger systems signal than any montage.

## Release Timing, Platforms, and Separating Fact From Hype

Release information is where trailer coverage gets messy. Aggregator sites, AI-generated "leak" videos, and repost accounts all compete for the same clicks, and most of them are guessing.

| Source type | Reliability | What to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| Official Blizzard blog and patch notes | Highest | Treat as confirmed |
| Official YouTube channel | High for content, low for unstated dates | Check the description, not the thumbnail |
| Established gaming outlets with press access | Medium to high | Cross-check two independent outlets |
| Datamined files and strings | Medium for mechanics, low for dates | Useful for systems, unreliable for scheduling |
| Anonymous leak accounts | Low | Enjoy it, don't plan around it |
| AI-narrated "trailer breakdown" videos | Very low | Often fabricated frame by frame |

Practical habits that save you from bad information:

- **Check the uploader before the content.** A trailer reposted by a fan channel is not an announcement.
- **Read the video description.** Platforms, dates, and disclaimers live there, not in the thumbnail text.
- **Look for a rating box or legal line.** Regional rating logos appear on official cuts only.
- **Verify against the publisher.** Bookmark [Blizzard's official Diablo site](https://diablo.blizzard.com/) and check it whenever a date starts trending.
- **Distrust round numbers.** "Coming this holiday" is a marketing phrase; a specific day is a commitment.

If a launch date hasn't been stated by the publisher, the honest answer is that it's unannounced — no matter how confidently a forum post says otherwise.
