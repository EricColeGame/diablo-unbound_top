## Diablo Unbound Playable Characters: The Confirmed Lineup

Four classes, one box, and a whole lot of loot. The Diablo Unbound playable characters were the first concrete detail Blizzard and Glass Cannon Unplugged put on the table when they revealed the game at Spiel Essen in October 2026. This isn't a new digital action RPG — it's a cooperative "dungeon battler" for one to four players — which means the Diablo Unbound playable characters you choose from shape every single session, not just your personal build.

The confirmed roster is the Barbarian, the Rogue, the Sorceress, and the Paladin. That's four classes drawn from across the franchise's history rather than from one game's lineup, which fits the studio's stated goal of pulling "from the broader history of the franchise."

For the full reveal timeline and quotes from Glass Cannon Unplugged CEO Jakub Wiśniewski, [Polygon's Diablo: Unbound announcement report](https://www.polygon.com/diablo-unbound-announcement-blizzard-glass-cannon-games/) is the most complete write-up so far.

## Barbarian, Rogue, Sorceress, and Paladin: How Each Class Plays

Every class ships with its own skill tree, its own abilities, and its own progression path. That's the single most important sentence in the announcement, because it means the four Diablo Unbound playable characters aren't cosmetic reskins of one another.

### Barbarian

The Barbarian is the safest assumption in the box: a front-line bruiser built to absorb pressure and hit back harder. In a cooperative dungeon battler where hordes of monsters "never let up," a character who can stand in the doorway and take hits has obvious value. Expect a build that trades finesse for raw staying power.

### Rogue

The Rogue brings speed and precision. If the Barbarian holds the line, the Rogue is the class that punishes enemies for clustering badly or leaving an opening. Rogues historically reward players who like calculated risks, which pairs neatly with the game's stated risk-versus-reward loot system.

### Sorceress

The Sorceress is your elemental answer to crowd problems. Area damage and control effects matter more in a cooperative game where four players share a board than in a solo power fantasy, so a well-timed Sorceress turn can define an entire dungeon level.

### Paladin

The Paladin is the class that makes a party feel durable. Whether that arrives as auras, defensive buffs, or healing, the Paladin is the natural pick for the player who enjoys keeping everyone else alive — and the natural glue for a four-player table.

| Party need | Best fit | Backup option |
|---|---|---|
| Soaking monster damage | Barbarian | Paladin |
| Single-target burst | Rogue | Sorceress |
| Clearing groups | Sorceress | Barbarian |
| Keeping the party standing | Paladin | Barbarian |
| Recovering from bad rolls | Paladin | Rogue |

## Building Your Character: Skill Trees, Loot, and Respec

Progression is where the Diablo Unbound playable characters diverge the most from a typical board game one-shot. The game runs on two layered systems: deck-building and dice-rolling. Your class determines the starting shape of both, and the choices you make between dungeons reshape them further.

The loop is structured. Each dungeon run takes roughly 60 to 90 minutes. Between runs, players enter a Town Phase where they upgrade skills, manage loot, and respec. A full act — three dungeon levels plus a boss — runs about three to four hours, and the campaign spans three acts.

| System | What it does | Where it happens |
|---|---|---|
| Skill tree | Unlocks class abilities and shapes your options | Town Phase |
| Deck-building | Adds, removes, and upgrades your cards | Between and during dungeons |
| Dice-rolling | Resolves attacks and skill effects | During dungeons |
| Randomized loot | Drops gear with risk-scaled rewards | Dungeon levels |
| Respec | Lets you rebuild a character mid-campaign | Town Phase |

The respec option deserves attention. In most campaign board games, a bad early build haunts you for twenty sessions. Here, the Town Phase gives you an out, which makes experimenting with the Diablo Unbound playable characters far less punishing — and makes a four-player group more willing to try unusual compositions.

Beyond the campaign sits Torment, an endgame mode where you can face any boss in its ultimate form, tuned for a party of fully geared characters. It's the mode that will ultimately decide which class builds hold up.

| Content unit | Approximate length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single dungeon | 60–90 minutes | One level plus encounters |
| Full act | 3–4 hours | Three dungeon levels and a boss |
| Full campaign | Multi-session | Three acts total |
| Town Phase | Short interlude | Skills, loot, respec |
| Torment | Variable | Endgame boss rematches |

## Which of the Diablo Unbound Playable Characters Should You Pick?

If you're playing solo, pick the class whose fantasy you actually enjoy — you'll be running it for a full campaign, and there's no one to cover your weaknesses. At a four-player table, spread your roles instead of stacking damage.

| If you enjoy... | Start with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Being the wall | Barbarian | Straightforward, forgiving, always useful |
| High-risk plays | Rogue | Rewards timing and calculated gambles |
| Puzzle-solving the board | Sorceress | Control and area effects reward planning |
| Supporting the team | Paladin | Durability plus party utility |
| Learning the rules first | Barbarian or Paladin | Fewest moving parts to track |

A few practical tips before your first session:

- **Don't finalize your build in dungeon one.** The Town Phase respec exists precisely so you can course-correct.
- **Talk about loot before you flip it.** Randomized drops create friction in cooperative games; agree on a claim order early.
- **Play the risk dial as a team.** Greater risk means greater reward, but one reckless player can sink a whole run.
- **Rotate who reads the rules.** Player experience with cooperative dungeon battlers consistently shows that one rules-lawyer carrying the table burns out fast.

## FAQ

**How many Diablo Unbound playable characters are there?**

Four have been confirmed: the Barbarian, the Rogue, the Sorceress, and the Paladin. Each has its own skill tree, abilities, and progression, and more classes are planned through stretch goals and expansions.

**Can you play Diablo Unbound solo?**

Yes. The game is designed for one to four players, so every class has to function as a complete solo option as well as part of a party. That's one reason the respec system matters so much.

**Will more Diablo Unbound playable characters be added later?**

Glass Cannon Unplugged has said additional classes will come via stretch goals and future expansions, but hasn't named them. The Q1 2027 Gamefound campaign is the next place to watch.

**Do the classes play differently from each other?**

They're designed to. Each of the four Diablo Unbound playable characters has a distinct skill tree and ability set, and the game layers deck-building on top of dice-rolling so your card pool changes as your character grows.
